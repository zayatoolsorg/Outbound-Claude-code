# Proof Library — Zaya case studies and how to use them

Every piece of Zaya proof-of-work the system can reference. Outreach may only
describe a project using wording supported by its entry here. Add new projects
using the template at the bottom.

---

## How to match proof to a prospect

For every qualified prospect, answer:

1. Is any existing Zaya work relevant?
2. Which case study is most relevant?
3. Why? Match on the **creative problem** first and the industry second.
4. Should it appear in the **first message**, or only **after they reply**?
5. Which single piece should be recommended?

Record the answers in `master.csv` (`Relevant Proof`, `Proof Relevance`,
`Recommended Portfolio Piece`) and in the lead card.

### Relevance scale

| Relevance | When |
|---|---|
| High | Same creative problem, and a similar kind of company |
| Medium | Same creative problem in a different industry, or the same industry with a different problem |
| Low | Only shows general craft or taste |
| None | Not relevant |

### Timing rule of thumb

- **High** relevance → may be mentioned lightly in the first message.
- **Medium** → usually after they reply, unless it makes the opening sharper.
- **Low / None** → don't mention in the first message.

### How to mention proof

Proof supports the message; it isn't the pitch. Connect it to the prospect's
situation, and describe **only what the case study shows Zaya did**.

Avoid:
- "Check out our Block Agency case study."
- Leading with the case study before the observation about *their* brand.
- Implying scale that isn't real ("our international clients", "brands worldwide").
- Describing a project as a different type of work than it was (e.g. calling a
  brand identity project "video production" or "OOH campaigns").

## Case studies

### CS-001 — Block Agency

**Source of truth:** 7 screenshots of the case study page on zayaproductions.com,
provided by the founder on 2026-09-28. Copies are saved in
`system/proof-assets/cs-001-block-agency/` (numbered in the order provided). The
site itself is blocked from this environment.

**Case study URL:** **NOT CONFIRMED.** It isn't visible in the screenshots. Ask the
founder. Never guess. (The page is labelled "Case study (01)" under the
"Case studies" section of the Zaya site.)

#### FACTS: directly visible in the screenshots

**What Block Agency is** (as described on Zaya's case study page)
- "An independent out-of-home agency, headquartered in Dubai and buying across
  60+ markets." [2]
- Offices in Dubai, London and Hong Kong. [6: Guidelines]
- Location tag on the page: Dubai. [1]
- Instagram handle: @blockagencyooh. [5]
- Website: blockagency.io. [6]
- Tagline shown on the page, attributed to "Block Agency": "We turn the world's
  streets into your stage." [7]

**Case study headline** [1]
- "Repositioning global OOH as a discipline of strategy, culture, and visibility."

**The brief / creative problem** [2]
- "The work was already on the best screens in the world, from Piccadilly Circus to
  New York. The brand wasn't. It looked like a media vendor, and they needed it to
  read like the specialist they are."

**The idea / creative approach** [3]
- "A billboard gets three seconds, so the brand had to land in one."
- "We built the identity the way Block builds a campaign: for distance, for speed,
  for a city that isn't paying attention."
- Oswald set tall and tight; a single burnt orange against matte black and paper
  white; a wordmark with enough clear space to survive any screen.
- "The same rules then carried into a website that sells the streets before it
  sells the service, and a social system that treats every post like a placement."

**What Zaya made** (the "What we made" section) [6]
| # | Deliverable | As described |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Identity | Wordmark and brand element, clear-space rules, a three-colour palette (burnt orange, matte black, paper white), Oswald + Inter type pairing |
| 02 | Website | blockagency.io, "designed around the work itself": selected work, cities, capabilities and The Feed (Block's own industry intelligence) |
| 03 | Social | "A post and motion system for LinkedIn and Instagram", with campaign breakdowns and market notes "built to be read at a scroll" |
| 04 | Guidelines | A brand guide that keeps the system consistent across offices in Dubai, London and Hong Kong |

**Social approach** [5]
- "A feed built like a media plan."
- Recurring series: "The Global 5" ranks the sites worth buying in each region;
  city posts put one screen in context; every cover follows the same system (bold
  Oswald headlines, one burnt orange highlight, the Block wordmark top left).

**Website visuals** [4]
- Mockups captioned "(II) Website, home, work, case study and capabilities".
- Pages shown include: a home page ("We're independent. So we answer to one person
  — you"), a city list (Los Angeles, Dubai, London, New York, Singapore, Paris,
  Riyadh), a work grid, a campaign case-study page and a capabilities page
  (Data-led planning, Buy, Programmatic, Measure).

**Results / outcomes:** **none shown.** No metrics, KPIs, traffic, follower,
revenue or business outcomes appear anywhere in the screenshots.

#### REASONABLE CREATIVE OBSERVATIONS (judgement, not fact)

- This is a **brand repositioning and identity project**, extended into web,
  social and guidelines. It is not a video production, 3D or OOH ad campaign
  project.
- The core problem is **"the brand undersells the work"** (vendor → specialist).
  That's the same gap Zaya looks for in Lane A prospects, so the case study
  shows Zaya's thesis in practice.
- The craft on show is strategic, systematic design: an identity built from the
  client's own medium (billboards), and social treated as a system, not one-off
  posts.
- The motion element is limited to a "post and motion system" for social. It shows
  motion design within a brand system, not standalone motion, 3D or film work.
- The page itself is premium and editorial, so the case study also works as a
  sample of Zaya's taste.

#### NOT VERIFIED: never claim without the founder confirming

- The case study URL.
- Any results, metrics or business impact of the project.
- That Zaya **built/developed** the website (the screenshots say "designed").
- That Zaya produces Block's ongoing social content, or which posts/reels Zaya
  made (the screenshots show a *system*; the reels in the Instagram mockup may or
  may not be Zaya's).
- That Zaya created any **OOH/billboard advertising** or campaign creative for Block
  or its clients.
- Any video production, filming, 3D, VFX or brand film work for Block.
- Project dates, duration, team or budget.
- That Block is a current/ongoing client. (The founder has said Zaya currently
  works with Block; the screenshots don't show it. This is founder-stated, so
  don't put it in outreach as a claim like "our client", beyond "we worked
  with".)
- Any relationship with the brands and campaigns visible in the website mockups
  (e.g. Beyond, Passo, Asana, Danube and other names on Block's work grid).
  **These are Block's clients and campaigns shown on Block's website, not Zaya
  clients.** Never name them as Zaya work.
- Whether the quote "We turn the world's streets into your stage" is a testimonial
  about Zaya. It reads as Block's own tagline. **Don't present it as a testimonial.**

#### Claims the outbound system MAY make

- "We worked with Block Agency, an independent OOH agency headquartered in Dubai."
- "We repositioned their brand / rebuilt their identity so it reads like a specialist
  rather than a media vendor."
- "We designed their identity, website, social system and brand guidelines."
- "We built a post and motion system for their LinkedIn and Instagram."
- "The brief was that their work was on some of the world's best screens but their
  brand didn't match it." (Keep close to the case-study wording.)
- "There's a case study on our site." (Link only once the URL is confirmed.)

Example phrasings (fit to the prospect; don't copy verbatim):
- "We had a similar problem with Block Agency in Dubai: their work was on the best
  screens in the world, but their brand read like a media vendor. There's a case study
  on our site if it's useful."
- "This reminded me of the identity and social system we built for Block, an
  independent OOH agency. Same challenge: making the brand match the work."

#### Claims the outbound system must NOT make

- "Global client portfolio", "international clients", "clients worldwide".
- "We produced OOH campaigns / billboards / DOOH content for Block" or "our work has
  run in Piccadilly Circus / Times Square". (The *Block's* work is on those screens,
  not Zaya's.)
- "We've worked with [any Block client or brand visible in the mockups]."
- Any results ("increased leads", "grew followers", etc.).
- "We shot / filmed / produced video for Block", "3D for Block".
- "We built blockagency.io" (say "designed", unless the founder confirms build).
- Presenting "We turn the world's streets into your stage" as client praise.
- "Block works with us across Dubai, London and Hong Kong" (the guidelines cover
  those offices; nothing shows Zaya works with each office).

#### Relevance by prospect type

Match on the creative problem: **a company whose brand, website or social doesn't
match the quality of its actual work or product**.

| Prospect type | Relevance | Default timing | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| OOH / DOOH / media agencies and media owners | **High** | First message (light) | Same industry, same kind of problem |
| Advertising, creative, media-buying, experiential or specialist agencies whose own brand/social looks generic | **High** | First message if the angle is their own brand; otherwise after reply | Agency brand undersells its work: the exact brief |
| B2B / professional / premium service firms that "look like a vendor" (consultancies, architecture, real estate developers' corporate brands, tech/SaaS, fintech) | **Medium–High** | Case by case | Same repositioning problem, different industry |
| Companies that just rebranded or are about to, or are expanding internationally with multiple offices | **Medium** | After reply, unless the angle is identity/consistency | Identity + guidelines across offices |
| Brands needing a social *system* (recurring series, consistent covers, LinkedIn + Instagram) | **Medium** | After reply | Social system deliverable |
| Agencies approached for **production partnership** (motion, 3D, post, editing, VFX) | **Low–Medium** | After reply | Shows taste and ad-industry fluency, but not production/post/3D craft |
| Consumer brands needing video, brand films, product content, 3D or performance ads (e.g. skincare, fashion, F&B, automotive) | **Low** | Don't lead with it | Different deliverable; proves design thinking, not production |
| Hospitality, luxury retail, lifestyle brands whose gap is content/video | **Low** | Don't lead with it | Same as above |

**Conflict check (flag to the founder, don't decide alone):** OOH agencies in
direct competition with Block (especially in the UAE, UK or Hong Kong) are the
highest-relevance audience, but they're also the most likely conflict of interest.
Treat them under qualification gate G5 until the founder sets a rule.

#### Gap this reveals in the proof library

CS-001 proves **brand identity, web design, social systems and brand guidelines**.
It doesn't prove video production, brand films, 3D, post-production, editing or
performance creative, which are central to the Lane A offer and the Lane B
production-partner pitch. Case studies covering that work are the most valuable
additions to this library.
